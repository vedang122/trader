## Synthesis

No candidate advanced or dropped on merit — none existed. The only judgment
that mattered was **M&M: hold, or blind-exit an unverifiable stop?**

Held, on five grounds, weighted in this order:

1. **It was not actually available.** `engine.execute` calls `data.last_price`
   at entry; with the feed 429'd the order dies at the engine door. Sending the
   execution-trader would have bought a REJECT, not an exit.
2. **A stop is a price contract, not a time contract.** It obliges exit when
   price is *observed* below 3295. Firing blind converts a conditional
   obligation into an unconditional one — that is not honouring the stop, it is
   replacing it.
3. **Bounded downside.** Risk to stop is ₹108.33 on 1 share, 1.08% of equity;
   even a −10% tail costs ₹340 against a −₹300 daily breaker that had used ₹0.
4. **Cost asymmetry.** A round-trip on ₹3,403 paid on zero information is
   cost-negative, and costs are this desk's declared first adversary at ₹10k.
5. **Circumstantial only, and not treated as a quote:** a broad up-day
   (Sensex +788) and a bullish M&M sentiment tag lower — without disproving —
   the odds that a stop 3.2% below entry was breached.

What I weighed most: (1) and (2). Even if I had wanted out, there was no door;
and I would not want the journal to record a blind exit as if it were
discipline. What was close: nothing here was close, but the *next* cycle is —
the obligation to check M&M against 3295 before anything else, and if it did
close below, to journal that as **a stop delayed by the outage, not a
discretionary hold**. I am writing that down now precisely so I cannot relabel
it later.

**Convergent finding.** Both analysts, independently, hit the same structural
critique: the regime's "5-of-6 risk-on" tally double-counts one driver. The
honest independent-cluster count is ~3 (Hormuz/crude, US-Europe tape reversal,
domestic vol compression). The scorer treats correlated inputs as independent
votes, so it *overstates* regime confidence, and a Hormuz reversal would remove
more of the score at once than 5-of-6 implies. Direction still looks right; the
method is what's flawed. Flagged for /debrief.

**Architecture gap.** Stops here are poll-enforced — evaluated only when a cycle
runs *and* the feed answers. `status.alerts` was empty this cycle not because
nothing breached but because nothing could be evaluated, and an empty alert list
under a dead feed is indistinguishable from one under a healthy feed. That
deserves a human decision, not an agent patch.
